# GHOST//ROOT — Collection Bible

Version 0.1.0 · canonical design proposal · 2026-09-07

## 1. Thesis

**3,333 identities survived a network that erased itself. Each is evidence that someone was here.**

Name: **GHOST//ROOT**. Symbol: **GHRT**, pending clearance. Tagline: **You were never supposed to find them.**

GHOST//ROOT is a portrait collection about the right to remain unknowable. Its imagined future makes a present anxiety tangible: when machines can generate a person, classify a person, and erase a person, what makes an identity yours?

The target collector appreciates a striking avatar first and discovers the fiction second. Security engineers, archivists, speculative-fiction readers, digital artists, and fashion-minded internet communities are natural creative audiences; this is a positioning hypothesis, not measured demand. Nobody must code, speculate, or perform daily engagement to belong.

The portrait is the product. The archive gives it history. Participation adds memory. Owning a GHOST confers no real administrator privilege, security credential, copyright ownership, financial claim, or promise of future revenue.

## 2. Positioning and name

The core visual promise is **composure under corruption**. GHOSTS look like evidence photographs of people an institution cannot classify. They are intimidating through stillness, elegant through proportion, and mysterious through deliberate absence.

The wordmark keeps both slashes: `GHOST//ROOT`. In long-form prose use “GHOST//ROOT” for the project, “a GHOST” for an identity, and “ROOT” for the fictional network. Display token names as `GHOST//0001` through `GHOST//3333`; actual integer token IDs are 1–3333.

The symbol is `GHRT`, not an associated fungible token. Clear trademarks, domains, social handles, Discord names, and marketplace names before public identity lock. Proposed names and handles are not availability claims.

The project is difficult to execute well because its material rules, anatomy, image composition, fiction, and technical state system must agree. Its moat is accumulated authorship and trust, not a claim that a collar or glitch can be monopolized.

## 3. Premise and canon

In 2037, ROOT disappeared from the internet. No shutdown logs survived. No source repository could be authenticated. No administrator acknowledged responsibility.

In 2044, recovery workers found three isolated archive shards inside decommissioned infrastructure. Each held 1,111 identity records. None contained a valid creation timestamp. Every face carried the same interrupted collar and a line across the eyes that investigators called the **witness seam**.

The count 3,333 comes from these three recoverable shards, not a claim that it is an optimal sales quantity. There are no surprise replacement shards inside this edition. The practical benefit is an achievable art-review surface with room for several recognizable families. If the budget cannot support it, reduce the supply before selling or announcing a binding mint configuration.

Public mystery: were the identities prisoners, operators, or the network itself? Authorial truth for season one: ROOT separated its witness, erasure, and permission functions to protect identities from an administrator purge. The three Genesis entities disagree because each preserves only one function's account. Reveal this truth in pieces; do not retcon trait scarcity to manufacture plot twists.

## 4. Tone, language, and collector identity

Tone: precise, restrained, unsettling, humane. Technical words describe something: a checksum verifies bytes; it does not decrypt them. A hash is not a secret. Story transmission timestamps are fictional and explicitly separated from real incident notices.

Collectors are **Witnesses**. They recover identities; they do not enlist in a hacker army. A nonholder can be a Witness through reading, solving, translating, or creating. Wallet addresses need not become public profiles.

| --- | --- |
| GHOST | One recovered identity, represented by one token |
| ROOT | The vanished network; uppercase access label is separately defined |
| Archive | Public collection browser and narrative record |
| Shard | One of three story sources; not an extra token or rarity multiplier |
| Witness seam | Interrupted horizontal line connecting identity and evidence |

| Incident | A published story chapter; never a concealed actual security issue |

Adoptable phrases: **GHOST ONLINE. / I WAS HERE. / KEEP THE BREAK. / NO SIGNAL IS STILL A SIGNAL. / ACCESS DENIED. / STAY ROOTED.**

Use “You were never here” as institutional propaganda in the fiction. The collectors' reply is “I was here.” That tension gives the brand a human voice beyond terminal status messages.

Never use fabricated partners, anonymous team claims that imply credentials, investment language, follower-count flexing, fake breach alerts, or fake countdowns. Utility copy stays literal: access to specified art and story features, subject to published availability.

## 5. Visual identity

The signature is a **broken collar + witness seam + asymmetric silence**. The collar opening is always on the viewer's right. The seam crosses the eye line, breaks on the same side, and is thin enough to read as calibration rather than a censor bar.

One camera, one crop, one studio. Heads turn about 8 degrees toward the viewer's left while maintaining an almost direct gaze. The face occupies roughly half the image height. Shoulders are monumental; hands stay outside the frame. A hard upper-left key catches bone ceramic, carbon composite, and forensic surface detail; a soft opposite edge separates the silhouette from an almost black archive field.

Primary materials are bone ceramic, matte carbon, smoked glass, restrained oxidized metal, and living or reconstructed tissue. Human faces span visibly different fictional adults and skin tones, with equally careful lighting. Human appearance, gender presentation, race, age, or disability is never a rarity score or a corruption metaphor.

Palette: archive black `#080B0D`, graphite `#20282C`, bone `#DED8C9`, frost `#9AAFB2`; vermilion `#D75B48` is the signal accent. Pale violet `#9585AD` marks phase interference. Keep luminous color under approximately 5% of the portrait. Gold is not a rarity material.

Typography: condensed grotesk wordmark direction, readable neutral sans for prose, tabular monospace for data. Suggested production candidates are IBM Plex Sans Condensed, IBM Plex Sans, and IBM Plex Mono; verify and archive license files before shipping. Draw the wordmark intentionally, including both slashes. Final font selection is an art-production gate.

Symbols: interrupted ring; two offset archive slashes; three unjoined vertical bars for the shards; checksum boxes. No copied government seals, corporate logos, skull emblems, green code rain, hoodies, random guns, fantasy gold crowns, or neon city backgrounds.

### What must never change

1. The collar opening and seam break remain on the viewer's right.
2. The same camera, face scale, crop, and key-light direction govern the edition.
3. The eye region, jaw silhouette, and collar remain legible at 64 pixels.
4. Corruption occupies controlled regions; it never becomes visual clutter across the whole face.
5. All classes preserve the recognizable collar and seam, including Genesis exceptions.
6. A common portrait receives the same material and composition finish as a legendary.
7. Origin traits, band, entity, and access never change after release.
8. Text and cryptographic fingerprints are rendered from real data outside the portrait; unique noise and serial numbers cannot disguise duplicated art.

## 6. The four entities

| Entity | Count | Identity and silhouette |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| HUMAN | 1,400 | Living tissue with intentional reconstruction; natural asymmetry and a grounded jaw |
| SYNTHETIC | 1,200 | Manufactured personhood; segmented cranial geometry and precise material joins |
| HOLLOW | 600 | A preserved shell with genuine negative space behind the face; absence has depth |
| SPECTER | 133 | An identity reconstructed from incompatible observations; a controlled double contour and volumetric gap |

These are fictional origin classes, not moral ranks. SPECTER is not simply a translucent HUMAN. HOLLOW is not a human face with black eye sockets. Each needs its own base geometry and occlusion masks.

Access distribution: USER 2,400; ADMIN 720; SYSTEM 180; ROOT 33. Access expresses original network permissions only. It grants no present software permissions or community voting power.

## 7. Edition and distribution

The edition contains **3,300 generative portraits + 30 individually art-directed legendary portraits + 3 Genesis 1/1s = 3,333**. The 30 legendaries each get an authored composition, not ten copies of three costumes. Ten archetypes provide three distinct legendary interpretations each.

| Allocation | Count | ID policy and disposition |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Genesis archive | 3 | IDs 1–3; disclosed archive multisig; custodial policy published |
| Founders/development | 66 | IDs 4–69; disclosed addresses and 180-day no-transfer commitment; no claim of on-chain vesting |
| Artists/contributors | 99 | IDs 70–168; named work agreements, addresses opt-in where legally appropriate |
| Community exhibition/grants | 33 | IDs 169–201; archive custody until awards; published criteria |
| Allowlist | 1,110 | Paid eligibility for nonreserved IDs; one allowance per selected wallet |
| Public | 2,022 | Initial paid capacity; unused allowlist capacity rolls in after its window |
| Total | 3,333 | Reserves are included, never additional |

The 198 non-Genesis reserves contain 108 common, 54 uncommon, 26 rare, and 10 epic portraits. All 30 legendaries enter paid inventory. Genesis custody is plainly disclosed; community participants can interact with Genesis stories without owning those tokens. Selection and awards must not be sold as investment opportunities.

Wallet cap: five mints per wallet in each phase — five early and five public, enforced by two independent on-chain counters (10 combined maximum per wallet). This limits addresses, not people. No system can infer one-human-one-wallet merely from these rules. Tokens transferred in do not consume mint allowance; tokens transferred out do not restore it.

The proposed mint is **visible-art, select-an-identity**, with a flat planning price of 0.004 ETH. Selection is first confirmed transaction; display transaction-ordering competition honestly. There is no random-reveal fairness claim. Price is provisional until costs and prefunding are reviewed, then fixed at deployment. Full art and all state variants are inspectable before payment. Narrative revelations remain staged.

Band is fixed by Architecture. Access is correlated with it and must not be counted twice in a combined rarity score. State progression does not promote band, create ROOT access, or create additional supply. Common DORMANT and common ROOTED must both be satisfying endpoints for collectors.

## 9. Evolution and cultural participation

`DORMANT → ACTIVE → COMPROMISED → ROOTED`

Every transition is voluntary and requires the current holder. ROOTED is reachable by a USER-access common; a ROOT-access Genesis starts DORMANT. COMPROMISED means the character's fictional record encountered conflicting evidence. Never present it as the connected wallet being compromised.

Season one is finite. Initialization recognizes presence; the breach chapter reveals that evidence can disagree; rooting means the holder chooses to preserve that disagreement. Every state has a finished portrait and metadata file committed before mint. A public contract selects among those files. Website lore can develop, but it cannot overwrite the edition's origin or substitute uncommitted art.

Puzzle participation earns archive credits and early chapter eligibility, not stronger financial claims. All holders obtain state access after a published fallback time. Nobody has to buy a second token, burn a token, reveal their legal identity, or solve a cybersecurity challenge to keep their portrait.

## 10. Physical and expansion policy

ROOT access cards are optional art objects: charcoal substrate, a cut interrupted ring, printed short serial, and a clearly identified official verification URL. A passive NFC URL is copyable. It is never a private key, an ownership proof, or an authentication credential. Link a card to a token only through an optional wallet-signed session, and allow unlinking.

Do not collect shipping information until an actual funded physical release exists. Keep it off-chain, separate from public profiles, and delete it under a published retention policy. Secure-element authentication would require a separate design and hardware review. Lost cards do not change token ownership.

Season two can add publications, installations, commissioned art, or an archive anthology after funding and demand are demonstrated. It cannot silently enlarge this collection or invent mandatory paid utility.

## 11. Rights and delivery promise

Recommended rights direction: holder display and personal adaptation of their portrait, with separately drafted commercial-use terms; the project retains collection marks, shared lore, and character bible copyright. Final license text, collaborator assignments, model/tool terms, and jurisdiction-specific obligations require qualified review. No legal conclusion is claimed here.

The launch promise should be limited to finished art, the deployed finite-state behavior, published archive content, and funded operational commitments. Budget art, engineering, independent review, and at least 12 months of storage/operations before selling. Royalties are not guaranteed revenue. A project that cannot fund its published commitments at low sell-through must narrow them before launch.
